# Task: Rebrand and Rebuild the David's Barbershop Website for Booksy Bookings and Paid Ad Campaigns

## Context and Assumptions
<task_context>
- **Source inputs:**
  - Brief: a voice-style transcript covering the goals, features, branding, ads, and research request.
  - Live site: https://davidsbarbershop84.com, which is the current site and the source of existing content.
  - Branding reference: a photo of the current branding, which the user will send separately.
  - Pattern reference: the Upper East Side Watch Repair site, which sets the bar for the parallax video and the design system.
- **Background:** David's Barber Shop has been on the Upper East Side since 1972. A father and son run it, and it is known for classic barbering and fair prices. The current site lists:
  - Services: haircuts from $22, royal shaves at $25, and beard trims at $15.
  - Hours: Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 7:00 pm, Sunday 9:00 am to 6:00 pm, closed Saturdays.
  - Testimonials covering many years.

  The owners want a full rebuild: a new brand system recreated from scratch but recognizably related to the current brand, barber profiles that link to Booksy, admin-managed services and prices, and landing pages built for paid campaigns. [Source](https://davidsbarbershop84.com)
- **Why this matters:** The site exists to turn paid and organic traffic into Booksy bookings. The core message is authentic, family-owned, legacy craftsmanship at $30 a cut, against roughly $55 elsewhere in Manhattan. Some barbers have cut famous athletes, and that is the proof point. Convey authenticity through heritage, the family story, and the neighborhood. Leave ethnic or religious labels out of all copy.
- **Campaign target:** The current campaign spends $300 on each of 3 barbers, $900 in total, and needs at least 15 Booksy bookings. That means a cost of $60 or less per booking, or 5 bookings per barber.
  - The $60 cost per booking is twice the $30 ticket price, so the campaign pays off only through repeat visits. Report repeat bookings from ad-acquired clients wherever the data allows.
  - 15 bookings is far below what ad platforms need to optimize on bookings alone. Meta's learning phase, for example, wants about 50 optimization events per week. Plan to optimize on a higher-funnel proxy event, such as a click through to Booksy, and feed real bookings back to the platforms as they come in.
- **Definition of done:** All of the following are true:
  - The rebranded site is live on the production domain.
  - Admins can add, edit, and remove barbers and services without touching code.
  - Every barber profile opens Booksy booking.
  - Per-barber and offer landing pages exist.
  - Call and booking conversion events reach the ad platforms, and a pipeline feeds actual Booksy bookings back to them.
- **Urgency:** No deadline was stated. Treat this as a full overhaul with launch gated on the finish lines.
- **Scope:**
  - In scope:
    - Brand system and assets, including vinyl and window decal files.
    - Site design and build.
    - Admin.
    - Booksy integration.
    - Generated parallax video background.
    - UES and NYC competitor research with a gap analysis.
    - Ad landing pages.
    - Conversion tracking and the auto-optimization setup.
  - Out of scope:
    - Running or buying the ad campaigns themselves.
    - Printing or installing the vinyls.
    - Replacing Booksy as the booking system.
  - If you see a better approach, say so in one sentence and continue as asked.
- **Assumptions made** (high impact first):
  - Assumed: the haircut price is now $30, and the $22 on the live site is outdated. Basis: the brief states "$30 per cut." Impact if wrong: **high**, because the entire ad message depends on this number. Confirm all current prices with the owners before launch.
  - Assumed: the full Booksy Public API is not available on day one. Basis: it is a partner-only REST API with RSA-key/JWT auth. Partners get keys from Booksy after contacting them, with no self-serve signup. Impact if wrong: **high**, because API access would enable live availability and a booking webhook. Build on the embeddable widget and profile links first, and apply for partner access in parallel. [Source](https://repuso.com/developers/platforms/booksy-reviews-api/) [Source](https://github.com/api-evangelist/booksy/blob/main/apis.yml)
  - Assumed: famous-client photos and names are not cleared for advertising. Basis: New York's right-of-publicity law (Civil Rights Law §§50–51) restricts commercial use of a person's name or likeness without written consent. Impact if wrong: **high**, because it creates legal exposure. Also verify the spelling of "Mike Judges."
  - Assumed: "normally $55" needs documented substantiation. Basis: comparative price claims in ads must be truthful and provable. Impact if wrong: **high**.
  - Assumed: the new site uses the same stack and hosting as the UES Watch Repair site. Basis: the brief says "similar to how we did it." Impact if wrong: medium. If that codebase is not accessible, choose a stack through R1.
  - Assumed: the ad platforms are Google Ads and Meta. Basis: these are the default for local service businesses. Impact if wrong: medium.
  - Assumed: prices and services shown on the site are managed in the site's admin, and Booksy remains the source of truth for availability and the actual booking. Basis: the widget cannot pull service data, and the API is gated. Impact if wrong: medium. Add a sync later if partner API access is granted.
  - Assumed: the color direction is still open. Basis: the user asked whether we decide it. Impact if wrong: low. Present three directions with a recommendation and let the owners choose.
</task_context>

## Research (Before Any Implementation)
<research_phase>
- **Tracks selected:** R1 through R5. All of them apply because the project covers branding, new libraries, ad-claim compliance, and an existing site plus a reference codebase.
- [ ] **R5: existing site and reference codebase**
  - [ ] Crawl every page of https://davidsbarbershop84.com. Capture copy, prices, hours, address, phone, testimonials, images, and the full URL list, which you will need for 301 redirects.
  - [ ] Note internal inconsistencies, such as "established 1972" against the "84" in the domain. Add them to the list of owner questions.
  - [ ] Read the Watch Repair codebase in full: stack, design tokens, parallax video implementation, admin, and deployment. Record what can be reused directly.
  - [ ] Review the current branding photo when it arrives, and list the elements to carry forward: wordmark shape, colors, iconography, and signage style.
- [ ] **R2: competitor research and gap analysis**
  - [ ] Analyze at least 8 Upper East Side barbershops and at least 8 well-regarded barbershops elsewhere in NYC. For each, capture:
    - Haircut price
    - Booking platform
    - Barber profile depth
    - Hero treatment
    - Calls to action
    - How reviews are shown
    - Gallery
    - Gift cards
    - Memberships
    - Kids or senior pricing
    - Walk-in policy
    - Instagram integration
    - Local SEO signals
  - [ ] Write `docs/research/competitors.md`. Include a comparison table, screenshots, and a "what we're missing" list ranked by expected effect on bookings.
  - [ ] Collect dated, sourced UES and Manhattan haircut prices to substantiate the "~$55 elsewhere" claim. Change the claim to match what the data shows.
- [ ] **R1: solution selection**, with 3 to 5 candidates each, scored on fitness, maintenance in the last 6 months, documentation, license, and advisories:
  - [ ] Stack and CMS/admin, if the Watch Repair stack doesn't fit.
  - [ ] AI video generation for the parallax hero.
  - [ ] A/B testing for landing pages.
  - [ ] Server-side conversion delivery: Meta Conversions API and Google Ads offline or enhanced conversions.
- [ ] **R3: official documentation**
  - [ ] Booksy widget: business-specific code is found under Business Settings → Online Booking → Your Website. It requires a JavaScript-enabled site, and Booksy offers no help with implementation. [Source](https://support.booksy.com/hc/en-us/articles/23480739244434-How-to-add-a-booking-widget-to-my-website-WordPress-Joomla-and-other-CMS-Platforms)
  - [ ] Booksy Public API: endpoints cover resources (staff), services, schedules, appointments, and an appointment webhook. Requests are versioned through the `Accept` header, and the US base URL is `https://us.booksy.com/public-api/us/`. Read https://docs.booksy.com/ and draft the partner access request. [Source](https://github.com/api-evangelist/booksy/blob/main/apis.yml)
  - [ ] Find out whether Booksy supports links or widget parameters that preselect a specific staff member, and whether it supports GA4 or Meta pixel tracking inside its hosted flow. Record the answer with evidence and choose a fallback.
  - [ ] Read the GA4, Google Ads, and Meta Conversions API documentation, with exact versions, and document the chosen patterns.
- [ ] **R4: compliance and claims**
  - [ ] Right of publicity: obtain written consent from each famous client before using their name or photo in ads or on the site. Until consent exists, use generic phrasing such as "trusted by pro athletes."
  - [ ] Comparative pricing: tie the "$30 vs ~$55" copy to the R2 evidence file.
  - [ ] Privacy: add a privacy policy and cookie consent that covers the ad pixels.
  - [ ] Accessibility: meet WCAG 2.2 AA. Include a pause control for the autoplaying video (WCAG 2.2.2) and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
</research_phase>
